"What changed?"

It's the first question every engineer asks when something breaks. And it's surprisingly hard to answer when you're managing a fleet of robots receiving over-the-air updates.

Yesterday, three robots started failing navigation. Is it the update you pushed this morning? A coincidence? Something else entirely? Without deployment tracking integrated into your observability, you're guessing.

## The Debugging Nightmare

Picture this scenario:

- **Monday 9:00 AM**: You push OTA update v2.3.1 to your fleet
- **Monday 11:00 AM**: Operations reports three robots failing to navigate
- **Monday 11:30 AM**: Two more robots show the same failure
- **Monday 12:00 PM**: You're still trying to figure out if it's the update

Questions racing through your mind:
- Did the failing robots receive the update?
- When exactly did they receive it?
- Are robots that didn't receive the update also failing?
- Is this a rollout issue or a code issue?

Without integrated deployment tracking, answering these questions requires:
1. Checking your OTA system for deployment status
2. Cross-referencing with robot IDs
3. Pulling failure logs with timestamps
4. Manually correlating everything in a spreadsheet

<Callout type="warning" title="The Cost of Manual Correlation">
Teams report spending 2-4 hours per incident just determining whether a deployment caused the issue. That's before any actual debugging begins.
</Callout>

Should you rollback? Ferronyx provides data to decide:

- **Failure severity**: Are these critical failures or minor issues?
- **Failure rate**: What percentage of updated robots are affected?
